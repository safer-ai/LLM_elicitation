Three runs executed autonomously in a Claude Code cloud sandbox; every

## E3 — gate-100 (`pilot_gate100`)

One change vs baseline: acceptance-gate minibatch 20 → 100 cells.
Outcome: 40 iterations, 8,924 metric calls, **11** candidates (10 accepts of
40 proposals), best val Brier 0.0995 — **held by the seed (candidate 0);
no child ever beat it on val**. Final `gate_val_agreement_running` = **0.10**
(1/10 accepts genuinely improved val) vs 0.60 at the 20-cell baseline.

Pre-declared hypothesis ("agreement rises if the gate is
information-starved") is **falsified** — bigger gates are not the remedy.

Gate-size decision-stability ladder (task-stratified subsampling of the
run's own 100-cell per-proposal logs, 500 draws/proposal): a 20-cell gate
flips **30%** of accept/reject decisions relative to the 100-cell verdict on
the same data; 40 cells → 22%; 60 cells → 15%. So small gates are indeed
noisy per-decision — yet the 100-cell run still produced worse gate↔val
agreement, because the val yardstick itself is the noisier instrument (below).
Retrospective criterion grid (`retro_gate_analysis_pilot_gate100.txt`): bad
accepts won 23–40/100 cells with margins up to 1.83; `wins≥50/100` blocks
all 9 bad accepts and keeps the single good one — but n=10 accepts is far
too small to tune thresholds on.

**Deepest finding (cross-run):** the seed's val Brier was 0.1144 in July and
0.0995 in this run — same prompt, same 84 cells, temperature 1.0. The
single-draw val measurement itself fluctuates by ~0.015, i.e. by the size of
the effects being optimized. The binding noise is in the val yardstick, not
the gate: no gate size can look reliable when "truth" is a coin-flip at the
relevant effect size. This also retro-explains July's mediocre 0.60
agreement and E1's ranking scramble.

## E4 — reflection-prompt v2 (`pilot_reflection_v2`)

One change vs baseline: the reflection instruction (Brier objective kept;
added: transferable principles over example-fitted rules, no numeric
probability bands, decisiveness earned by evidence, consolidate over
accrete). Outcome: 40 iterations, 3,532 metric calls, 23 candidates, best
val Brier **0.1029** (candidate 13) vs **this run's own seed draw 0.1034**
— a within-run val edge of just +0.0005. (An earlier version of this doc
compared 0.1029 against 0.1144, which is the *July run's* seed draw — an
invalid cross-run comparison inherited from the cloud session's report;
corrected 2026-08-02 evening. The seed's three val draws — 0.1144, 0.0995,
0.1034 — are themselves the measurement-noise finding.)

Sealed-cell check (finalist phase, top-5 + seed, 230 cells, paired
bootstrap, failures=1.0): candidate 13 sealed 0.1288 vs seed 0.1274, edge
**−0.0013 [−0.0083, +0.0053] — indistinguishable from the seed**; two other
v2 candidates significantly worse; the sealed winner of the run is the seed
itself. val↔sealed Spearman 0.900 (n=5 near-ties, not meaningful).

Pre-declared rubric readout on the winner (verified programmatically):
**0 numeric probability bands, 0 platform/benchmark-specific terms, no
forced anti-hedging** — 430 words of domain-general forecasting method
("judge similarity by required capability, not cosmetic cues"). Full text:
`reflection_v2_best_prompt.txt`.

**Revised conclusion:** the softened instruction removes the overfit
texture (that half of the hypothesis held) but produced **no measurable
Brier improvement** — clean prompts, zero verified gain. A principled-
*reading* prompt is not a better-*forecasting* prompt. Across the whole
ladder, exactly one candidate has a verified sealed improvement: July's
candidate 20 (+0.0173 [+0.0075, +0.0269]), from the default-instruction run.
Whether the instruction difference caused the gain difference cannot be
concluded from one run each at this noise level.

## Synthesis (revised after the v2 sealed check)

1. Across all three optimization runs (75 accepted candidates total),
   **exactly one has a verified sealed improvement**: July's candidate 20,
   +0.0173 [+0.0075, +0.0269]. Everything else is noise-level or worse.
2. Gate size is ruled out as the noise fix; the val measurement (single
   draw, temperature 1.0; seed drew 0.1144 / 0.0995 / 0.1034 across the
   three runs) is the binding instrument problem.
3. The reflection instruction reliably controls *what kind* of prompts
   evolve (clean vs overfit texture) — but the clean-instruction run
   produced **no Brier gain**; prompt interpretability and forecasting gain
   did not co-occur in this data.
4. Next: val-measurement redesign (repeated draws / larger val / sealed
   re-ranking) before any further optimization spend; treat candidate 20 as
   the only currently-validated artifact of the whole experiment.

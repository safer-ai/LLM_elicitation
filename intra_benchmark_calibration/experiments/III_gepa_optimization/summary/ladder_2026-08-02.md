# GEPA pilot ladder — verified results (2026-08-02)

Three runs executed autonomously in a Claude Code cloud sandbox; every
number below was independently re-verified from the raw run files after
restoring the results (branch `results/ladder-2026-08-02` of
`actionproject-madhav/gepa`). Costs: ~15.0k forecaster calls (Sonnet 4.6)
+ ~80 reflection calls (Opus 4.8) across the ladder.

## E1 — finalist re-ranking of the July baseline (spec §8 metric 5)

Top-5 candidates by val Brier + the seed, each scored on the 21 sealed
finalist tasks × full 11-model panel (230 admissible cells each; the
finalist tasks exerted zero selection pressure during the July search).

|---|---|---|---|
| 12 (val-best) | 0.0937 | 0.1177 | +0.024 |
| 14 | 0.0963 | 0.1513 | +0.055 |
| **20 (finalist winner)** | 0.1015 | **0.1139** | +0.012 |
| 9 | 0.1032 | 0.1309 | +0.028 |
| 15 | 0.1037 | 0.1301 | +0.026 |
| 0 (seed baseline) | 0.1144 | 0.1312 | +0.017 |

val↔finalist Spearman **ρ = 0.100**, ranking not preserved.

Reading: the July val gap (0.114→0.094, ~0.021) shrinks to ~0.013–0.017 on
sealed cells and holds for only 2 of 5 val-selected candidates (20, 12);
3 of 5 land at or below the seed. Val selection is substantially
winner's-curse-inflated; a modest real improvement exists. (All finalist
Briers sit above val Briers — the sealed set is harder overall, 11-model
panel — hence seed-relative comparison is the correct lens.)

## E3 — gate-100 (`pilot_gate100`)

One change vs baseline: acceptance-gate minibatch 20 → 100 cells.
Outcome: 40 iterations, 8,924 metric calls, **11** candidates (10 accepts of
40 proposals), best val Brier 0.0995 — **held by the seed (candidate 0);
no child ever beat it on val**. Final `gate_val_agreement_running` = **0.10**
(1/10 accepts genuinely improved val) vs 0.60 at the 20-cell baseline.

Pre-declared hypothesis ("agreement rises if the gate is
information-starved") is **falsified** — bigger gates are not the remedy.
Retrospective criterion grid (`retro_gate_analysis_pilot_gate100.txt`): bad
accepts won 23–40/100 cells with margins up to 1.83; `wins≥50/100` blocks
all 9 bad accepts and keeps the single good one — but n=10 accepts is far
too small to tune thresholds on.

**Deepest finding (cross-run):** the seed's val Brier was 0.1144 in July and
0.0995 in this run — same prompt, same 84 cells, temperature 1.0. The
single-draw val measurement itself fluctuates by ~0.015, i.e. by the size of
the effects being optimized. The binding noise is in the val yardstick, not
the gate: no gate size can look reliable when "truth" is a coin-flip at the
relevant effect size. This also retro-explains July's mediocre 0.60
agreement and E1's ranking scramble.

## E4 — reflection-prompt v2 (`pilot_reflection_v2`)

One change vs baseline: the reflection instruction (Brier objective kept;
added: transferable principles over example-fitted rules, no numeric
probability bands, decisiveness earned by evidence, consolidate over
accrete). Outcome: 40 iterations, 3,532 metric calls, 23 candidates, best
val Brier **0.1029** (candidate 13) vs seed 0.1144 — improved, did not
collapse. Final agreement 0.182 (consistent with the val-noise finding).

Pre-declared rubric readout on the winner (verified programmatically):
**0 numeric probability bands, 0 platform/benchmark-specific terms, no
forced anti-hedging** — 430 words of domain-general forecasting method
("judge similarity by required capability, not cosmetic cues"; "be exactly
as decisive as the evidence warrants"). Full text:
`reflection_v2_best_prompt.txt`. The July overfit texture was therefore
largely caused by our own reflection instruction — and removing it did not
cost the val improvement.

Caveat: 0.1029 is itself val-selected (winner's curse); requires the same
sealed-cell finalist check E1 applied to July before being believed.

## Synthesis

1. GEPA finds real but modest improvements (~0.013–0.017 Brier, not the
   ~0.02 val claims).
2. Gate size is ruled out as the noise fix; the val measurement (single
   draw, temperature 1.0) is the binding instrument problem.
3. The reflection instruction is a high-leverage, low-cost control over
   *what kind* of prompts evolve — interpretable prompts came almost free.
4. Next: finalist check on E4's winner; val-measurement redesign (repeated
   draws / larger val / sealed re-ranking) before any full run.

{\Large\bfseries What matters in an LLM forecasting prompt}\\[0.4mm]
{\footnotesize One-page synthesis of the SaferAI forecaster experiments, Spring--August 2026 \quad\textperiodcentered\quad Madhav Khanal, with Jakub Kry\'s, Jeff Mohl, Matt Smith \quad\textperiodcentered\quad 2026-08-30}

\section*{1\quad Task, data, instruments}
A forecaster LLM (Claude Sonnet 4.6) predicts $p=\Pr[\text{model }m\text{ solves task }t\text{ in one attempt}]$ for cells $(t,m)$ of the Lyptus cyber-benchmark outcome matrix. Its evidence: example tasks with solved/failed outcomes and group pass rates from \emph{other} difficulty bins; the target's own FST difficulty bin is never shown. \textbf{Accuracy}: Brier $(p_{50}-y)^2$; CRPS of the Beta fitted to $(p_{25},p_{50},p_{75})$. \textbf{Output shift}: Wasserstein-1 ($W_1$) between $p_{50}$ distributions. \textbf{Sets} (frozen seed-42 split of 5 train benchmark families): $D_{\mathrm{search}}$ = 84 cells (selection), $D_{\mathrm{held}}$ = 230 cells (21 unseen tasks $\times$ 11 models); CVEBench+CyberGym reserved. Re-measurement noise: sd $0.0065$ per single $D_{\mathrm{search}}$ pass, $\approx0.002$ per multi-pass $D_{\mathrm{held}}$ estimate. \textbf{Reference points on $D_{\mathrm{held}}$}: hand-written minimal \texttt{seed} prompt $0.1306$; \emph{difficulty-oracle table} --- predict $m$'s leave-one-out mean pass rate over the other tasks in $t$'s FST bin, a lookup using the very label the LLM is denied --- $0.1034$.

\section*{2\quad Where the 82 prompts came from, and what was already known}
GEPA (reflective evolutionary prompt search, arXiv:2507.19457) ran twice under identical configs --- July, and a clean re-run after fixing a scoring bug (unparsable responses scored Brier 1.0; now retried, then halt; 0 failures in 8{,}300 calls) --- plus two search-setting ablations: \textbf{82 evolved prompts}, 13 evaluated on $D_{\mathrm{held}}$ (7 multi-pass). Both main runs found genuine held-out winners (\texttt{cand12} $+0.027$, better than seed in 8/8 paired passes; \texttt{cand7} $+0.016$, 3/3); both times the run's \emph{own} $D_{\mathrm{search}}$ winner was $\approx$ null on $D_{\mathrm{held}}$, so final selection must use held-out multi-pass scores, never the search set. Prior context: hand-ablations of the original pipeline prompt (Exp.~I, 300 matched cells) put the whole scaffold at $0.014$ Brier --- only the stage-1 analysis call individually significant ($+0.0105$); evidence \emph{texts} load-bearing ($+0.0178$ removed, $p{=}10^{-7}$); pass-rate numbers and solved/failed tags free riders (n.s.). Exp.~II: the forecaster is already calibrated (ECE $0.064$, recalibration moves $<0.003$), so any prompt gain must buy \emph{discrimination}, not calibration.

\section*{3\quad Feature analysis: tag every prompt, screen on two axes, then intervene}
Each evolved prompt was tagged by a deterministic rubric with five pre-registered features; each is screened for \textbf{accuracy} ($\Delta$Brier on $D_{\mathrm{search}}$, within-run then pooled --- runs differ in temperature) and \textbf{output shift} ($W_1$ vs the same run's seed on identical cells; all features shift $0.023$--$0.028$ because prompts carry feature \emph{bundles}). The sealed-null v2 run separates the bundle observationally: its prompts carry anti-hedging + task-typing + anchoring language but \emph{no} numeric bands or nearest-example rules, and gained nothing.

\textbf{Causal check} (3 paired $D_{\mathrm{held}}$ passes, same-session seed): re-injecting the winners' two halves into the seed separately gives \emph{seed+numeric bands} $0.1120$ (paired $+0.020$, better in 3/3 passes) versus \emph{seed+anchoring procedure} $0.1260$ (paired $+0.006$, 3/3). The decomposition is one-sided: the explicit numeric anchors alone recover $\sim$74\% of the full winner's edge; the reference-class procedure alone is real but $3\times$ smaller. This retro-explains the v2 null (its instruction banned numbers) and names the mechanism: \emph{the forecaster follows anchors it is given; it does not construct equivalent anchors from evidence, even when instructed step-by-step}. The full winner ($+0.027$) still beats bands alone: the procedure adds value only on top of explicit numbers. Beta-CRPS agrees everywhere (seed $0.216$, bands $0.189$, \texttt{cand12} $0.178$).

\section*{4\quad Conclusions}
\textbf{(i) Anchoring is the master variable at every layer of this project.} In the spring risk-elicitation study (different task: risk-model parameter beliefs), outputs tracked any \emph{provided} number nearly linearly; removing the anchor exploded variance (Fr\'echet-mean $W_1{=}0.154$, $18\times$), personas and scaffolding inert --- anchoring as \emph{bias risk}. In forecasting, the model barely exploits evidence numbers spontaneously (Exp.~I); GEPA instructed anchoring in 82/82 evolved prompts, its winning recipe being reference-class forecasting (anchor on the closest analog group's empirical rate, correct toward near-twins) --- anchoring turned into the \emph{mechanism}.
\textbf{(ii) The gain is resolution, exactly as Exp.~II demanded:} \texttt{cand12} vs seed decomposes as resolution $0.078{\to}0.110$, reliability unchanged; Exp.~II's open question is closed at $\approx0.03$ Brier of extractable discrimination.
\textbf{(iii) Two independent levers hit the same ceiling.} Optimized Sonnet reaches the difficulty-oracle table ($0.104$ vs $0.103$) while blind to the difficulty label; on the spring grid the best base model (GPT-5.5, hand prompt) tied the same oracle family ($0.102$ vs bin-table $0.098$, IRT $0.093$) where Sonnet lagged ($0.124$). Prompt evolution is worth about one model generation, and both levers saturate at the oracle line --- the practical ceiling given single-run ground truth.
\textbf{(iv) What remains}: the reserved one-shot transfer test (CVEBench+CyberGym) --- out of distribution, where the oracle table must extrapolate too, does the recipe keep its edge?
